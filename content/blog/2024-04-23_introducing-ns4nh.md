KDE welcomes the newly-funded, two-year project *Nachhaltige Software Für Nachhaltige Hardware* (NS4NH; English: 'Sustainable Software For Sustainable Hardware'), a KDE Eco initiative funded by the Federal Environment Agency (UBA) and the Federal Ministry for the Environment, Nature Conservation, Nuclear Safety and Consumer Protection (BMUV).

## Environmentally-Friendly Software?!?

Software is a frequently unacknowledged, even hidden yet significant factor for sustainability. Software determines a hardware's energy consumption and minimum system requirements. It determines how long a device can remain safely in use. With software running on everyday devices, from coffee machines to smartphones, from trains to drones, the role of software in keeping hardware in use and out of the landfill grows more critical every day.

For consumers, the environmental harm is out-of-sight and out-of-mind. Yet the environment is registering its effects, from the C02 pumped into the atmosphere from hardware production and the energy consumed in its use; or in the landfills that receive our discarded devices at their end of life -- as well as the air, soil, and waters around them, not to mention the people and animals.

Software and hardware are inextricably linked: When software is repairable, hardware can stay in service longer. When users are guaranteed autonomy in managing the software they use, they are able to lower the energy and performance demands from the devices they depend on. [Sustainable software](https://eco.kde.org/blog/2023-05-10-what-is-sustainable-software/) is increasingly recognized as critical for our collective future. It is one reason why KDE adopted "[Sustainable Software](https://community.kde.org/Goals/Sustainable_Software)" as a community goal in 2021.

Software consumes energy. Software can render your device obsolete. But you can opt in to sustainability by opting green, today! How? By installing independent and sustainable Free & Open Source Software (FOSS) for sustainable hardware use.

## The Most Environmentally-Friendly Device Is The One You Already Own

In 2015, Achim Steiner, former Executive Director of the UN Environment Programme (UNEP), [warned](https://news.un.org/en/story/2015/05/497772) of the "tsunami of electronic waste rolling out over the world". In 2016, 44.7 million tonnes of e-waste were generated, equivalent to 4500 Eiffel Towers. By 2017, [United Nations University](http://www.unu.edu/media-relations/releases/ewaste-rises-8-percent-by-weight-in-2-years.html) determined e-waste to be the [fastest growing waste stream](https://www.weforum.org/reports/a-new-circular-vision-for-electronics-time-for-a-global-reboot/) in the world. The amount of e-waste hit 59.4 million tonnes in 2022, a 33% increase since 2016.

And the flow of e-waste continues to rise.

This is especially devastating when you consider the environmental and social harm caused by e-waste, in particular when e-waste is generated earlier than necessary because of premature obsolescence. The production and transportation of a device accounts for 50-80+% of its carbon footprint over its lifecycle. A [German Environment Report](http://www.uba.de/uba-info-medien/4316.html) estimates you’d need to use a computer for over 30 years before efficiency gains in newly-produced devices justify their purchase. In production, the extraction of rare earth metals consumes copious amounts of energy and takes place under miserable social conditions, often in the Global South.

For disposal, devices are typically returned to the Global South for end-of-life treatment, where they pollute the environment as toxic waste and cause enormous damage to workers' health or even death. That is, when they are properly disposed. Less than 20% of e-waste is actually recycled. And although e-waste makes up only 2% of the total waste in landfills, it contributes over [70% of the toxic waste](https://weee-forum.org/ws_news/international-e-waste-day-2021/).

Hardware obsolescence is all too often driven by software. When software support stops, devices are not safe to use -- continuing to use out-of-date software puts you at a security risk! In recent decades, new hardware has become more and more powerful, but software has become more inefficient and energy-intensive. Vendors often require buying a new device to support software updates. This is frequently driven by an economic –- not a technological –- logic. On [14 October 2025](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/lifecycle/faq/windows), the end of support for Windows 10 is [estimated](https://www.canalys.com/insights/end-of-windows-10-support-could-turn-240-million-pcs-into-e-waste) to make e-waste out of [240 million devices](https://www.reuters.com/technology/microsoft-ending-support-windows-10-could-send-240-mln-pcs-landfills-report-2023-12-21/).

But these devices do not need to be discarded -- they can remain in use with Free & Open Source Software. Use your device to the end of the hardware's, not the software's, operating life! You don’t need a new computer to get secure, up-to-date software -- you just need the right software.

## Bridging The Gap

There is a gap between consumers who indicate their desire for sustainable and repairable consumer goods, and those whose purchasing decisions are influenced by sustainability standards.

According the WWF-commissioned report, "[An Eco-wakening](https://www.worldwildlife.org/publications/an-eco-wakening-measuring-awareness-engagement-and-action-for-nature)", global interest in environmental harm and sustainable goods has been rising steadily from 2015 to 2021. This rise has been particularly steep in emerging markets, but it is also found in European countries. [One 2020 Eurobarometer poll](https://europa.eu/eurobarometer/surveys/detail/2228) found that [8 in 10 European consumers](https://europa.eu/eurobarometer/api/deliverable/download/file?deliverableId=72255) believe manufacturers should be required to make it easier to repair digital devices. A staggering 50% indicate that the reason they purchase a new device is due to performance issues or non-functioning software! Despite such high numbers, however, only [20-37% of consumers report](https://www.umweltbundesamt.de/sites/default/files/medien/1410/publikationen/uba_40jahreblauerengel_publikation_en_web.pdf) that their acquisition decisions are influenced by ecolabels such as the Blue Angel and the EU-Ecolabel.[^1]

[^1]: An exception appears to be the [European Union Energy Label](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_energy_label), which applies to refrigerators, dishwashers, washing machines, light bulbs, and other household appliances. Up to 85% of consumers report that this label influences their purchasing decisions.

How many of those consumers are aware that software determines hardware performance and for how long their devices can remain safely in use? How many understand the role software plays in hardware longevity and repairability? How many consumers know about Blue Angel eco-certification for software? Or how fulfilling the criteria -- with transparency and user autonomy being recognized as critical for sustainable software design -- will extend their device's operating life?

Free & Open Source Software guarantees, by design, transparency and user autonomy. FOSS puts users [in control](https://fsfe.org/freesoftware/index.en.html) by removing vendor dependencies: you can use your device how you want, for as long as you want! That means you avoid software bloat in order to increase hardware efficiency, or stop [unwanted data use and ads](https://groenlinks.nl/sites/groenlinks/files/2021-09/CE_Delft_210166_Carbon_footprint_unwanted_data-use_smartphones.pdf) from consuming energy unnecessarily. Autonomy to tailor the software for your needs means running software designed for the hardware you already have –- lean, efficient Free & Open Source Software runs on devices that are decades old!

## From BE4FOSS To NS4NH

From 2021-2023, the KDE Eco project *Blauer Engel Für FOSS* (BE4FOSS; English: 'Blue Angel For FOSS') had the goal of collecting and spreading information about the [Blue Angel criteria](https://www.blauer-engel.de/en/productworld/resources-and-energy-efficient-software-products) for sustainable software among Free & Open Source Software developer communities. The criteria for Blue Angel eco-certtification are aligned with FOSS values. During the project, [Okular](https://okular.kde.org/), KDE’s popular PDF and document reader, became the [first ever Blue Angel eco-certified software](https://eco.kde.org/blog/2022-03-16-press-release-okular-blue-angel/)! The BE4FOSS project culminated with the KDE Eco handbook "Applying The Blue Angel Criteria To Free Software", which you can read [here](https://eco.kde.org/handbook/).

Over the next two years, from 2024-2026, the project *Nachhaltige Software Für Nachhaltige Hardware* (NS4NH) will take all that KDE has learned and done for sustainable software directly to end users. A particular target group for the project is eco-consumers, i.e., those whose consumer behavior is driven by principles related to the environment, not just price or convenience. Through educational campaigns and workshops, the project aims to combat premature e-waste -- whether from software bloat or software-driven obsolescence -- with independent and sustainable Free & Open Source Software. Although the problem of e-waste is relevant for numerous large and small household appliances, IoT devices, and an increasing number of everyday products running software systems, the project's focus will be on desktop PCs, laptops, and, when possible, smartphones and tablets. We will set up info-stands at open-air fair-trade, organic, and artisinal markets, as well as sustainability events such as the [Umweltfestival](https://www.umweltfestival.de/) in Berlin on 28 April 2024. Here we will demo vendor-abandoned devices which are able not only to be used, but also enjoyed thanks to the power of FOSS. Installation workshops will give users the tools to keep devices they have at home in use for as long as they want.

Our motto: "The most environmentally-friendly device is the one you already own".

During the project, we will be reaching out to FOSS projects providing continued support for aging but functioning hardware in order to promote the amazing work they do. We believe the sustainability benefits of FOSS along with software eco-certification with the Blue Angel will be attractive to eco-oriented consumers and help drive a movement for more sustainable software/hardware. All the while, KDE Eco will continue measuring the energy consumption of Free & Open Source Software (for instance, using [KEcoLab](https://eco.kde.org/blog/2024-03-28-sok24-energy-measurement-lab/) with [emulation tools](https://eco.kde.org/blog/2024-02-20-sok24-wayland_support_kdeecotest/) like [KdeEcoTest](https://eco.kde.org/blog/2024-02-20-windows-kdeecotest-support/)) to build the best and most-efficient software for this and future generations.

Perhaps you'd like to get involved? People like you from around the world will make these campaigns and workshops a success. We would love to have you join us. Learn more here: https://eco.kde.org/get-involved/
