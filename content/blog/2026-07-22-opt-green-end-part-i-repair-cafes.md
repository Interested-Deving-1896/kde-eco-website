As May came to an end, so did KDE Eco's "Opt Green: Sustainable Software For Sustainable Hardware" (German: *Nachhaltige Software Für Nachhaltige Hardware*) project. Read the [announcement](https://eco.kde.org/blog/2024-05-29_introducing-ns4nh/) from 2024 at the project's start. It has been a full and exciting two years. Together with a great team, dozens of communities, and thousands of volunteers we really achieved a lot. Highlights include: We organized stands at [environmental festivals](https://floss.social/@be4foss/112332015705832479), [markets](https://floss.social/@be4foss/114710216026030872) and the [Long Night of the Sciences](https://floss.social/@be4foss/112683207939133955). We did outreach at [international conferences](https://floss.social/@be4foss/115150588729184584). We hosted install parties for [adults](https://floss.social/@be4foss/114748408438299075) and [children](https://floss.social/@be4foss/113708388646578981), as well as taught [upcycling](https://floss.social/@be4foss/116697191038161548) [courses](https://floss.social/@be4foss/112999467563097293) in high school. We published beautiful ads, [advertorials](https://web.archive.org/web/20260330152731/https://werde-magazin.de/stories/beitrag/opt-green-eine-nachhaltigere-digitale-welt-ist-moeglich/?_ppp=9ff391efeb), and [articles](https://floss.social/@be4foss/116798414401293997) in Free & Open Source Software (FOSS) and eco-consumer magazines, and even co-authored an [academic paper](https://floss.social/@be4foss/115547259601891579). We built new networks across FOSS and repair communities. Last but definitely not least, we [mobilized](https://floss.social/@be4foss/113713173732030500) volunteers worldwide to offer in-person Linux support in a [global campaign](https://floss.social/@be4foss/113424955829573330) against e-waste driven by Microsoft's software policies.

I only wish we had another two years!

I will not attempt to write a retrospective of everything the project did. Instead, I would like to focus on two aspects of the project which I believe will result in our main goals not only continuing, but thriving well beyond the project's end. This post will be published in two parts. Here, in Part I, I will present Opt Green's activities in networking and collaborating with Repair Cafés. In Part II, to be published soon, I will take a closer look at the End Of 10 campaign and what comes next after the unexpected two-year extension of Windows 10 support.

## Networking With Repair Cafés

Repair collectives provide a community space to fix stuff. There exist many groups offering workshops to make and repair things, such as [Hackerspaces, Makerspaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makerspace), [FabLabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fablab), and [Linux User Groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux_User_Groups). The focus here will specifically be [Repair Cafés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repair_Caf%C3%A9) , though. FOSS, and the role it plays in keeping computers in use after vendor support ends, is for the most part a new topic for these communities. Moreover, Repair Cafés generally serve a different demographic than Hackerspaces, Fablabs, LUGs, etc. Working with Repair Cafés provides an opportunity to reach people well outside the FOSS bubble.

Repair Cafés are a place to repair objects used in everyday life, such as clothing, electronics, bicycles, and so on. The first Repair Café was launched in Holland in 2009 by Dutch journalist Martine Postma. Since then thousands have popped up around the world with support from organizations such as [Repair Cafe International](https://www.repaircafe.org/) (started by Martine Postma), the [Restart Project](https://therestartproject.org/) (creators of Right To Repair and Open Repair Alliance), [iFixit](https://www.ifixit.com), [anstiftung](https://anstiftung.de/), among others.

### From Skepticism, To Support

The project's outreach to Repair Cafés in Germany started at the end of 2024 and early 2025. We were initially met with some skepticism. Software was simply not seen as being of interest to repair communities. Hardware, yes; anything related to real physical objects, sure. But software, not so much.

One of Opt Green's goals was to raise awareness about how software is as important to hardware longevity as physical components like batteries, cables, and screens. The repair motto "Repair Instead Of Throw Away" (in German: "[*Reparieren statt Wegwerfen*](https://anstiftung.de/praxis/reparieren)") was perfectly in line with Opt Green's goals. The project had even adopted mottos with similar messages: "The most environmentally-friendly device is the one you already own!" and "You don't need a new computer&mdash;you just need the right software!" So we kept contacting whomever we could.

The project's first breakthrough to the German repair community was at an online presentation in [March 2025](https://web.archive.org/web/20250405225518/https://www.reparatur-initiativen.de/post/das-ende-von-windows10-und-wie-man-pcs-und-laptops-danach-noch-nutzen-kann) organized with the repair organization [anstiftung](https://anstiftung.de/). In the talk we underscored how software [drives e-waste](https://www.reparatur-initiativen.de/files/kcfinder/posts/97597/2025-03-24-KDE-linux-anstiftung.pdf) along with other forms of energy and resource consumption. We showed how software repair&mdash;which FOSS licenses make possible&mdash;could extend the operating life of devices in order to reduce the environmental harm of hardware production, transportation, and disposal. 

Around 180 Repair Café volunteers participated … and we were pleasantly suprised to learn that a handful of volunteers were already offering FOSS support in their Repair Cafés.

The first meetup was such a success that together with anstiftung we organized a [follow-up webinar](https://www.reparatur-initiativen.de/post/das-ende-von-windows10-und-wie-man-pcs-und-laptops-danach-noch-nutzen-kann) in May with End Of 10 contributors Varut & Herbert. A heartfelt thank you to both for presenting! What a joy to see at the second event again over 100 Repair Café volunteers interested to learn how to install and use FOSS on their own devices. It seems that software is in fact of interest to repair communities!

As of July 2026, a little over one year after that second webinar, on the End Of 10 website there are 85+ places and 170+ events listed from Repair Cafés, Linux Cafés (i.e., repair cafés focused specifically on Linux), and similar organizations like IT-Cafés, Technik-Cafés, etc. (Once you include the Makerspaces, Hackerspaces, Fablabs, LUGs, and other initiatives that stepped up to help out, there are over 1180 places and events in total on the End Of 10 website&mdash;more on that in Part II.) Moreover, Repair Café International, an [End Of 10 supporting organization](https://endof10.org/#supporters), now lists 40 [Linux Repair Cafés](https://www.repaircafe.org/en/linux-repair-cafe/) on their website. And the Restart Project published their own "[End Of Windows 10 toolkit](https://therestartproject.org/end-of-windows-10-toolkit-for-repair-groups/)" to promote FOSS among repair groups as a way to keep unsupported Windows devices in use.

One End Of 10 contributor came up with a slogan which I feel captures this connection between repair and Free & Open Source Software beautifully, namely:

### Let's Bring FOSS and Repair Communities Together

In order to further strengthen the ties between FOSS and repair communities, the Opt Green project organized three in-person workshops from December 2025 to March 2026  with support from the funders UBA and BMUKN. The idea was to bring together local Repair Cafés, Fablabs, Linux User Groups, and related communities for a skills and information exchange. Each event took place in a different part of Germany: Hannover (middle), Nürnberg (south), and Dessau (east). Half of participants at each event were repair community members, half FOSS enthusiasts, while some belonged to both communities.

In the workshops a wide range of topics were covered, such as:

* How can the FOSS community support Repair Cafés, and vice versa?
* What are the barriers to adoption of Linux? What do new users need to succeed?
* After installation, how much support can Repair Cafés realistically offer? Would user support fit into regular opening times? Could more technical problems be directed to collaborators in local FOSS groups?
* How can Linux be promoted in local communities? What are the target groups? How does one reach them?
* What platforms are there for Repair Café volunteers to exchange tips and support each other?

And more.

Before the workshops, we also held an install event for Repair Café volunteers new to Linux. That way they could roll their sleeves up and get some hands-on experience.

Clearly there is not one right answer to any of the above questions, and just having two days for an exchange between locally-networked groups was as important as any one comment or reply. Even though there was, as always, room for improvement, the feedback after these workshops was overall very positive. If you live in an area with active Repair Cafés and Linux User Groups I strongly recommend organizing similar cross-community workshops in your town. Don't hesitate to be in touch if you want some tips!

You can read the minutes from all three workshops [here](https://invent.kde.org/teams/eco/opt-green/-/work_items/175#note_1364833) (Hannover), [here](https://invent.kde.org/teams/eco/opt-green/-/work_items/183) (Nürnberg), and [here](https://invent.kde.org/teams/eco/opt-green/-/work_items/184#note_1446930) (Dessau). Discussions from the Repair Café workshops also informed "Part III: Upgrading Devices With FOSS" in the new KDE Eco handbook "[Opting Green: Sustainable Software For Sustainable Hardware&mdash;And How Repair Collectives Can Bring Free & Open Source Software To Communities](https://eco.kde.org/opt-green-handbook/)". The handbook provides an overview of environmental harm driven by software and how FOSS provides a solution, including many tips for organizing install and support events. Check it out!

### After The Workshops

Since the last of the three workshops in March, several exciting updates have made it into our inbox.

We learned that four repair cafe volunteers from [*Repair-Café Wendelstein*](https://www.wendelstein-evangelisch.de/repaircafe) who attended the workshop have now started offering [Linux support](https://web.archive.org/web/20260718105937/https://www.wendelstein-evangelisch.de/repaircafe). Also, attendees from [*Reparatur Café Helmstedt*](https://reparatur-cafe-he.de/) have since organized two install events and one [Linux User Training](https://web.archive.org/web/20260721110842/https://reparatur-cafe-he.de/) in their café. The Repair Café at the neighborhood cultural center [*Haus Drei*](https://haus-drei.de/) in Hamburg now holds regular Linux installation workshops as well.

What is more, [*Repair Café Hilpoltstein*](https://www.repaircafe-hilpoltstein.de/), one of the groups that was already offering Linux support (their website is full of helpful information), has launched a [Discourse forum](https://forum.makes-hacks-hip.de/) (in German) to provide community support for Repair Café volunteers.

We also brought KDE Eco materials to the workshops to distribute them to interested groups around Germany. And it was great to see that *Repair Café Hilpoltstein*  had an [info stand](https://web.archive.org/web/20260718110459/https://www.repaircafe-hilpoltstein.de/blog/artikel/pfingstmarkt-in-hip-neues-aus-der-offenen-werkstatt) at a local market in May, featuring among other things the ["Environmentally-Friendly Software" leaflet](https://invent.kde.org/teams/eco/opt-green/-/tree/master/materials/leaflets/EN/current-version) ([German version](https://invent.kde.org/teams/eco/opt-green/-/tree/master/materials/leaflets/DE/current-version)) from the Opt Green project.

### Appendix: When Repair Is Not Possinle

If at some point you just need to buy new hardware, consider buying one of many Linux-supported devices on the market. That way, you can use the computer as long as the hardware keeps working. And with devices made for Linux you will have a better out-of-the-box experience, with less tinkering and fewer workarounds to get those pesky proprietary-software components working.

KDE [supporting members](https://ev.kde.org/supporting-members/) like [Slimbook](https://slimbook.com/),[Kubuntu Focus](https://kfocus.org/), [Tuxedo Computers](https://www.tuxedocomputers.com/), [framework](https://frame.work/) all sell hardware with native Linux support. Many offer hardware replacement parts and repair guides too, so you can easily swap out broken pieces when needed.

If you instead decide to buy a new computer with Microsoft pre-installled but plan to install Linux instead, check out the [Refund 4 Freedom](https://en.refund4freedom.org/) initiative. Here you can learn how to get a refund for that unused and unwanted Windows license you paid for.

If you buy used hardware, you may also want to head over to the [h-node project](https://h-node.org) to learn how well that hardware will support Free & Open Source Software.
