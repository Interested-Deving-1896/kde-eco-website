As May came to an end, so did the "Opt Green: Sustainable Software For Sustainable Hardware" (German: *Nachhaltige Software Für Nachhaltige Hardware*) project. Read the [announcement](https://eco.kde.org/blog/2024-05-29_introducing-ns4nh/) from 2024 at the project's start.

It has been a full and exciting two years. Together with dozens of communities and thousands of volunteers we achieved a lot, from organizing stands at [environmental festivals](https://floss.social/@be4foss/112332015705832479), [markets](https://floss.social/@be4foss/114710216026030872) and the [Long Night of the Sciences](https://floss.social/@be4foss/112648326204286577), hosting [install](https://floss.social/@be4foss/115129108712097415) [parties](https://floss.social/@be4foss/114748408438299075), doing outreach at [international conferences](https://floss.social/@be4foss/115150588729184584), and teaching [upcycling](https://floss.social/@be4foss/116697191038161548) [courses](https://floss.social/@be4foss/112999467563097293) in high school; to publishing ads, [advertorials](https://web.archive.org/web/20260330152731/https://werde-magazin.de/stories/beitrag/opt-green-eine-nachhaltigere-digitale-welt-ist-moeglich/?_ppp=9ff391efeb), and [articles](https://floss.social/@be4foss/116798414401293997) in FOSS and eco-consumer magazines, co-authoring peer-reviewed [academic papers](https://floss.social/@be4foss/115547259601891579), building new networks between FOSS and repair communities, not to mention [mobilizing](https://floss.social/@be4foss/113713173732030500) thousands to offer in-person Linux support in a [global campaign](https://floss.social/@be4foss/113424955829573330) against e-waste driven by Microsoft's software policies.

Here I will not attempt to write a retrospective of everything the project did. (The links included above are just to provide some examples.) Instead, this post, published in two parts, will focus on just two aspects of the project. These, I believe, will result in the project's main goals not only continuing, but thriving well beyond 2026.

In Part I, I will present Opt Green's activities in networking and collaborating with Repair Cafés.

In Part II, to be published soon, I will take a closer look at the End Of 10 campaign, and what comes next after an unexpected two-year extension of Windows 10 support.

## Networking With Repair Cafés

[Repair Cafés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repair_Caf%C3%A9) provide a community space to fix things. There are of course other initiatives offering workshops to make and repair things, such as [Hackerspaces, Makerspaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makerspace), [FabLabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fablab), and [Linux User Groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux_User_Groups). The focus here will be on Repair Cafés, though. Free & Open Source Software (FOSS)&mdash;and the role it plays in keeping computers usable with up-to-date software&mdash;is for the most part a new topic for this community. The Opt Green project aimed to change that.

Repair Cafés are a place to repair objects used in everyday life, such as clothing, electronics, bicycles, and so on. The first Repair Café was launched in Holland in 2009 by Dutch journalist Martine Postma. Since then thousands have popped up around the world with support from organizations such as [Repair Cafe International](https://www.repaircafe.org/) (started by Martine Postma), the [Restart Project](https://therestartproject.org/) (creators of Right To Repair and Open Repair Alliance), [iFixit](https://www.ifixit.com), [anstiftung](https://anstiftung.de/), among others. 

### From Skepticism, To Support

The project started outreach to Repair Cafés in Germany at the end of 2024 and early 2025. We were initially met with some skepticism. Software was simply not seen as being of interest to repair communities. Hardware, yes. Anything related to real physical objects, sure. But software, not so much.

One of Opt Green's goals was to raise awareness about how software is as important to hardware longevity as physical components like batteries, cables, and screens. The repair motto "Fix Instead Of Throw Away" (in German: "[*Reparieren statt Wegwerfen*](https://anstiftung.de/praxis/reparieren)") was entirely in line with Opt Green's goals. The project had even adopted a motto with a similar message: "The most environmentally-friendly device is the one you already own!" So we kept contacting whomever we could.

The project's first breakthrough was an online presentation to the German repair community through the repair organization [anstiftung](https://anstiftung.de/) in [March 2025](https://web.archive.org/web/20250405225518/https://www.reparatur-initiativen.de/post/das-ende-von-windows10-und-wie-man-pcs-und-laptops-danach-noch-nutzen-kann). We highlighted how software drives e-waste along with other forms of energy and resource consumption. We showed how software repair&mdash;which FOSS licenses enable&mdash;could extend the operating life of software-based devices to reduce the environmental harm of production, transportation, and disposal. Around 180 people participated.

We were pleasantly suprised to learn that some volunteers were already offering FOSS support in their Repair Cafés!

The first meetup was such a success that together with anstiftung we helped organize a [follow-up webinar](https://www.reparatur-initiativen.de/post/das-ende-von-windows10-und-wie-man-pcs-und-laptops-danach-noch-nutzen-kann) in May with End Of 10 contributors Varut & Herbert. A heartfelt thank you to both for presenting! What a joy to see over 100 Repair Café volunteers at the second event learn how to install and use FOSS on their own devices. For many this was their first introduction to Linux.

As of July 2026, just over one year later, there are 85+ places and 170+ events listed on the End Of 10 website from Repair Cafés, Linux Cafés (i.e., repair cafés focused specifically on Linux), and similar organizations like IT-Cafés, Technik-Cafés, etc. Repair Café International, one of Opt Green's contacts and [End Of 10 supporter](https://endof10.org/#supporters), now lists 40 [Linux Repair Cafés](https://www.repaircafe.org/en/linux-repair-cafe/) on their website. And the Restart Project published their own "[End Of Windows 10 toolkit](https://therestartproject.org/end-of-windows-10-toolkit-for-repair-groups/)" to promote FOSS among repair groups as a way to keep unsupported Windows devices in use.

It seems that software is in fact of interest to the repair community!

One End Of 10 contributor came up with a slogan which captures the connection between Repair Cafés and software perfectly: "FOSS Instead Of Toss!"

### Bringing FOSS and Repair Communities Together

In order to further strengthen the ties between FOSS and repair communities, the Opt Green project organized three workshops from December 2025 to March 2026  with support from the project funders UBA and BMUKN. The idea was to bring together Repair Cafés, Fablabs, Linux User Groups, and related communities for a skills and information exchange. Each event took place in a different part of Germany: Hannover (middle), Nürnberg (south), and Dessau (east). Half of participants at each event were repair community members and half FOSS enthusiasts.

In the workshops a wide range of topics were covered, such as:

* How can the FOSS community support Repair Cafés and vice versa?
* What online platforms are there for Repair Café volunteers to exchange tips and support each other?
* What are the barriers to adoption of FOSS? What are the risks? What do new users need to succeed?
* How much post-installation support can Repair Cafés offer? Should very technical issues be directed to local FOSS groups?
* How can Linux be promoted in local communities? Etc.

You can read the minutes for all 3 workshops [here](https://invent.kde.org/teams/eco/opt-green/-/work_items/175#note_1364833) (Hannover), [here](https://invent.kde.org/teams/eco/opt-green/-/work_items/183) (Nürnberg), and [here](https://invent.kde.org/teams/eco/opt-green/-/work_items/184#note_1446930) (Dessau). Before the workshops, we also held install parties for the Repair Café volunteers new to Linux. That way they could roll their sleeves up and get some hands-on experience.

Since March, several exciting updates have made it into our inbox.

We learned that four repair cafe volunteers from [*Repair-Café Wendelstein*](https://www.wendelstein-evangelisch.de/repaircafe) who attended the workshop have now started offering [Linux support](https://web.archive.org/web/20260718105937/https://www.wendelstein-evangelisch.de/repaircafe). Also, attendees from [*Reparatur Café Helmstedt*](https://reparatur-cafe-he.de/) have since organized two install events and one [Linux User Training](https://web.archive.org/web/20260721110842/https://reparatur-cafe-he.de/) in their community space. The Repair Café at the neighborhood cultural center [*Haus Drei*](https://haus-drei.de/) in Hamburg now holds regular Linux installation workshops.

Moreover, [*Repair Café Hilpoltstein*](https://www.repaircafe-hilpoltstein.de/), one of the groups that was already offering Linux support&mdash;their website is full of helpful information&mdash;has launched a [Discourse forum](https://forum.makes-hacks-hip.de/) in German specifically for Repair Cafés offering FOSS support.

We brought KDE Eco materials to the workshops to distribute them to interested groups around Germany. So it was great to see that *Repair Café Hilpoltstein* also had an [info stand](https://web.archive.org/web/20260718110459/https://www.repaircafe-hilpoltstein.de/blog/artikel/pfingstmarkt-in-hip-neues-aus-der-offenen-werkstatt) at a local market, featuring among other things the "Environmentally-Friendly Software" [leaflet](https://invent.kde.org/teams/eco/opt-green/-/tree/master/materials/leaflets/EN/current-version) ([German version](https://invent.kde.org/teams/eco/opt-green/-/tree/master/materials/leaflets/DE/current-version)) from the Opt Green project.

Moreover, the Repair Café workshops led to another KDE Eco collaboration. The Hilpoltstein group also organized an [info-meetup](https://floss.social/@be4foss/116720438501362487) for [FOSS in volunteer work](https://archive.is/2Fnyb) in the Nürnberg region in June. They are planning more outreach in the near future as well especially since Bavaria announced in June that [no new deals will be made with Microsoft, at least for now](https://www.br.de/nachrichten/bayern/korrektur-der-staatsregierung-vorerst-kein-deal-mit-microsoft,VLTSkXp).

It is exciting to see the FOSS and repair networks grow and connect. If you live in one of these areas, or know of Repair Cafés in your town or city, reach out to see how you can support their work!

### The "Opting Green" Handbook

Discussions from the workshops also informed the newly published handbook "[Opting Green: Sustainable Software For Sustainable Hardware&mdash;And How Repair Collectives Can Bring Free & Open Source Software To Communities](https://eco.kde.org/opt-green-handbook/)". The handbook provides an overview of environmental harm driven by software and how Free & Open Source Software (FOSS) provides a solution. Check it out!
